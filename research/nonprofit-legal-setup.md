# 1. Legal setup options

*Research checked October 17, 2026. Recommendations below are labeled separately from legal requirements. “Not found” means I could not verify the point from the sources reviewed.*

**The first decision is who will legally operate the project.** Incorporation, federal tax exemption, California tax exemption, and charitable registration are separate steps; incorporating alone does not establish tax exemption. [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/federal-tax-obligations-of-nonprofit-corporations), [California FTB](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/file/business/types/charities-nonprofits/help-with-charities-nonprofits.html)

## Comparing the three options

| Option | Legal and financial arrangement | Main tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| **School club or informal project** | A project can operate within an existing organization. Alternatively, an unincorporated association can itself qualify for exemption if it meets the applicable requirements; incorporation is not mandatory. Calling something a club does not establish its tax status. [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/life-cycle-of-a-public-charity-starting-out), [Minnesota Council of Nonprofits](https://minnesotanonprofits.org/resources-tools/starting-a-nonprofit/alternatives-to-starting-a-nonprofit) | **My assessment:** best for testing the website and workshops. Obtain written school approval covering activities, money, supervision, and use of its name. |
| **Fiscal sponsorship** | An existing charity accepts responsibility for a mission-aligned project or makes controlled grants to it. Eligible donations go to the sponsor, which must retain control over charitable funds. [Adler & Colvin](https://www.adlercolvin.com/blog/2012/10/15/what-is-fiscal-sponsorship/) | **My assessment:** strongest starting option if a sponsor accepts this project’s advocacy and interstate chapters. |
| **California nonprofit public benefit corporation + 501(c)(3)** | A separate corporation operates the project and undertakes its own governance, exemption applications, accounting, and required filings. [CalNonprofits](https://calnonprofits.org/resources/how-to-start-a-california-nonprofit/) | **My assessment:** appropriate once there is a durable team, funding, and a reason to operate independently. |

**Unincorporated does not mean unregulated.** California charitable-registration requirements can apply to unincorporated organizations holding or soliciting charitable assets. A school club’s particular treatment depends on its actual relationship with the school; this founder’s school policies were **not found** because no school was identified. [California Attorney General, FAQs 13–14](https://oag.ca.gov/charities/renewals)

## Fiscal sponsorship: Model A versus Model C

| Model | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Model A: comprehensive/direct project** | The project becomes part of the sponsor. Its leaders work as the sponsor’s employees or volunteers, and the sponsor has authority over the project and its funds. [Adler & Colvin](https://www.adlercolvin.com/blog/2012/10/15/what-is-fiscal-sponsorship/) |
| **Model C: preapproved grant relationship** | The project remains outside the sponsor. The sponsor receives charitable contributions, retains discretion over them, and makes grants for approved purposes, requiring expenditure reports. [Adler & Colvin](https://www.adlercolvin.com/blog/2012/10/15/what-is-fiscal-sponsorship/) |

Model C generally leaves more operational, tax, and liability responsibilities with the project; it should not be assumed to include insurance or comprehensive administration. The National Network of Fiscal Sponsors reports an average administrative allocation around **10%**, with considerable variation. That is a planning reference, not a universal price. [NNFS](https://www.fiscalsponsors.org/blog/10-questions-funders-should-ask-grantees-who-use-fiscal-sponsors)

## If choosing an independent California corporation: step by step

1. **Choose the mission, name, initial board, and agent for service of process.** Check both corporate-name availability and trademark conflicts. My suggested mission is public education about extreme-heat electricity protections, civic participation, and charitable advocacy—not merely “passing legislation.” The actual activities must match the exemption application. [CalNonprofits](https://calnonprofits.org/resources/how-to-start-a-california-nonprofit/), [IRS organizational requirements](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/organizational-test-internal-revenue-code-section-501c3)

2. **File Articles of Incorporation.** The Secretary of State lists the public-benefit form as **ARTS-PB**; its downloadable form is headed **ARTS-PB-501(c)(3)**. The standard filing fee is **$30**. Include the required charitable-purpose and asset-dedication provisions. [SOS forms and fees](https://www.sos.ca.gov/business-programs/business-entities/forms/corporations-california-domestic), [Articles form](https://bpd.cdn.sos.ca.gov/corp/pdf/articles/arts-pb.pdf), [IRS required provisions](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/organizational-test-internal-revenue-code-section-501c3)

3. **Adopt bylaws and organizational resolutions.** Address board selection, terms, meetings, quorum, officer authority, finances, and whether there are statutory voting members. Document officer appointments, the fiscal year, and banking authority. California requires a chair or president, a secretary, and a treasurer or CFO; the secretary/treasurer/CFO cannot concurrently serve as president or chair. [CalNonprofits](https://calnonprofits.org/resources/how-to-start-a-california-nonprofit/), [Corporations Code §5213](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/corporations-code/corp-sect-5213/)

4. **Adopt a conflict-of-interest policy.** I recommend disclosure, recusal, and recorded approval procedures for transactions involving directors, officers, or their families. The IRS encourages such a policy but explicitly says adoption is **not required to obtain exemption**. [IRS Form 1023 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1023)

5. **Obtain an EIN after legal formation.** Apply directly through the IRS; an EIN is **free**. It identifies the organization but does not itself establish exemption. [IRS application](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number), [IRS nonprofit guidance](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/obtaining-an-employer-identification-number-for-an-exempt-organization)

6. **File Statement of Information SI-100.** File within **90 days** of initial registration and **every two years thereafter**. The scheduled filing fee is **$20**. [SOS](https://www.sos.ca.gov/business-programs/business-entities/forms/corporations-statement-information)

7. **Register with the California Attorney General using CT-1.** Generally, register within **30 days after first receiving charitable property**, including money; do not wait for an IRS determination. The initial fee is **$50**. Follow the current checklist for governing documents and exemption materials. [Initial registration](https://www.oag.ca.gov/charities/initial-reg), [Registration timing](https://oag.ca.gov/charities/renewals)

8. **Apply for federal recognition using Form 1023 or 1023-EZ.** Both are submitted electronically through Pay.gov. Current user fees are **$600 for 1023** and **$275 for 1023-EZ**. [IRS fees and filing](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/frequently-asked-questions-about-form-1023)

   | Form | Eligibility and practical choice |
   |---|---|
   | **1023-EZ** | Complete the entire eligibility worksheet. Among its requirements: annual gross receipts must not exceed **$50,000** in the relevant past/projected three-year periods, and assets must not exceed **$250,000**. Other exclusions apply; meeting these financial limits alone is insufficient. [Instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1023ez) |
   | **1023** | Use when ineligible for EZ. **My recommendation:** consider the full application if counsel believes the advocacy program needs a detailed explanation, even if EZ is available. [IRS application guidance](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/frequently-asked-questions-about-form-1023) |

   Generally, filing within **27 months after the end of the formation month** allows approved exemption to date back to formation, subject to the applicable rules. [IRS instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1023ez)

9. **Obtain California FTB exemption separately.** Ordinarily use **3500A** with a qualifying federal determination letter effective from the start date; use **3500** when there is no federal exemption or in specified reinstatement situations. FTB’s ordinary application filing fees have been eliminated. Until FTB grants exemption, nonprofit incorporation alone does not establish California tax exemption. [FTB process](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/file/business/types/charities-nonprofits/index.html), [FTB fees](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/misc/3500-booklet.html), [3500A instructions](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/misc/3500a-instructions.html)

10. **Create the recurring filing calendar immediately.**

   | Filing | Typical requirement for a small public charity |
   |---|---|
   | **IRS 990-N** | Generally available when gross receipts are normally **$50,000 or less**, subject to exceptions. Due the **15th day of the fifth month** after year-end. [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/annual-electronic-filing-requirement-for-small-exempt-organizations-form-990-n-e-postcard) |
   | **California 199N** | Separate California e-Postcard, generally for organizations with gross receipts normally **$50,000 or less**, subject to exceptions and the “normally” calculation. Due the **15th day of the fifth month** after year-end. Larger organizations generally file Form 199. [FTB requirements](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/file/business/types/charities-nonprofits/annual-and-filing-requirements.html), [FTB Publication 1068](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/misc/1068.html) |
   | **AG RRF-1** | Annual renewal, normally due **four months and 15 days after year-end**. Current fee is **$25 when total revenue is below $50,000**, increasing with revenue. [AG](https://oag.ca.gov/charities/renewals) |
   | **AG CT-TR-1** | Required with RRF-1 when the organization is eligible for, and actually files, IRS 990-N. Otherwise, submit the required 990/990-EZ/990-PF copy. [AG](https://oag.ca.gov/charities/renewals) |
   | **SOS SI-100** | Biennial after the initial filing. [SOS](https://www.sos.ca.gov/business-programs/business-entities/forms/corporations-statement-information) |

**Basic initial filing arithmetic:** $30 articles + $20 SI-100 + $50 CT-1 + $275 EZ = **$375**, or **$700** using full Form 1023. These totals exclude insurance, professional help, banking, website costs, and later renewals. [SOS](https://www.sos.ca.gov/business-programs/business-entities/forms/corporations-california-domestic), [AG](https://www.oag.ca.gov/charities/initial-reg), [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/form-1023-and-1023-ez-amount-of-user-fee)

# 2. Minors: governance, contracts, banking, and safety

## Can a California minor be an incorporator, director, or officer?

The careful answer is that the cited general California nonprofit provisions contain **no express 18-or-older requirement**. That is different from finding a statute expressly authorizing minors in every role.

| Role | Statutory evidence |
|---|---|
| **Incorporator** | §5120 permits one or more “persons” to form the corporation and specifies who signs the articles; it states no minimum age. [Corporations Code §5120](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/corporations-code/corp-sect-5120/) |
| **Director** | §5047 defines directors as natural persons with governing authority and voting rights; it states no minimum age. [§5047](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/corporations-code/corp-sect-5047/) |
| **Officer** | §5213 specifies required offices and appointment rules without stating a minimum age. [§5213](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/corporations-code/corp-sect-5213/) |

**My reading:** these provisions do not categorically exclude a 16-year-old. An express California agency ruling confirming this founder’s eligibility for all three roles was **not found**. Youth-board guidance cautions against treating statutory silence as complete resolution of contracting, fiduciary-duty, and insurance questions. [Nonprofit Law Blog](https://nonprofitlawblog.com/youth-board-members-can-minors-serve-on-a-nonprofit-board/)

**My recommended arrangement:** preserve student leadership through a student executive team and advisory council, while recruiting competent adults for corporate governance, authorized signatures, and financial oversight. If incorporating, I would seek three independent adult directors; that is my governance recommendation, not a statement that California universally requires three. Advisory members without governing votes are not statutory directors merely because of their title. [Youth-board guidance](https://nonprofitlawblog.com/youth-board-members-can-minors-serve-on-a-nonprofit-board/), [§5047](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/corporations-code/corp-sect-5047/)

## Contracts

California minors **can make many contracts**, but generally possess a statutory right to disaffirm them before adulthood or within a reasonable time afterward, subject to exceptions. Therefore, “minors cannot sign contracts” is too broad. [Family Code §6700](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/family-code/fam-sect-6700/), [§6710](https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/family-code/fam-sect-6710/)

**My recommendation:** have an authorized adult officer or sponsor execute organizational contracts. Have counsel distinguish a signature made for the corporation from any personal guarantee; do not casually make a parent personally responsible for organizational debts.

## Bank accounts

There is no bank-independent answer established by the sources reviewed for a minor serving as a nonprofit account signer: **not found**. Banks impose their own opening and identification requirements; published business-account guidance requests identification, tax information, and organizational documents. [Chase business-account requirements](https://www.chase.com/business/banking/checking)

**My recommendation:** use a sponsor-controlled account or a corporation-owned account with adult authorized signers. Keep charitable money out of the founder’s personal account, require receipts, and have a second person review statements. HCB specifically offers financial administration for qualifying teen-led projects. [Internal-control guidance](https://www.councilofnonprofits.org/running-nonprofit/administration-and-financial-management/internal-controls-nonprofits), [HCB](https://hackclub.com/fiscal-sponsorship)

## Liability and insurance

**General liability** typically addresses bodily injury and property-damage claims; **D&O** addresses specified claims arising from organizational management and governance. One does not substitute for the other. Actual insured persons and exclusions depend on the policy. [Nonprofit Risk Management Center](https://nonprofitrisk.org/resources/what-basic-insurance-coverage-should-a-nonprofit-consider/)

**My recommendation:** before events, obtain written confirmation covering the sponsor/parent, chapters, volunteers, minor leaders, and relevant activities. Ask about abuse/molestation, transportation, and online-publication exposures. A reliable premium quote for this proposed organization: **not found**. Youth-director coverage should be confirmed with the insurer. [Youth-board insurance guidance](https://nonprofitlawblog.com/youth-board-members-can-minors-serve-on-a-nonprofit-board/), [Coverage guidance](https://nonprofitrisk.org/resources/what-basic-insurance-coverage-should-a-nonprofit-consider/)

## Child-safety policies

For organizations meeting California’s statutory definition of a **youth service organization**, Business and Professions Code §18975 requires specified abuse-identification/reporting training, background checks, and prevention policies. Policies must provide for outside reporting and, to the greatest extent possible, two mandated reporters when supervising or contacting children, subject to the statute’s exception. “Regular volunteer” includes an adult with direct contact exceeding **16 hours monthly or 32 hours annually**. [§18975](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-bpc/division-8/chapter-2-9/section-18975/)

**My recommended chapter policy:** cover adult screening, supervision, parent consent, transport, communications, photographs, incident reporting, and emergency contacts. Have the host determine statutory applicability and review each chapter state’s requirements before activities begin.

# 3. Lobbying rules for a 501(c)(3)

## The distinction that matters most here

For federal charity-tax purposes, **legislation includes state bills and ballot measures but excludes actions of executive, judicial, and administrative bodies**. Consequently, advocacy to a utility commission about implementing existing law is generally administrative advocacy rather than legislative lobbying. The classification depends on what action is actually being sought. [IRS lobbying guidance](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/lobbying), [Bolder Advocacy](https://www.bolderadvocacy.org/resource/administrative-advocacy-/)

**Federal tax classification and state lobbying disclosure are separate systems.** California can regulate attempts to influence administrative action even when the activity does not count as legislative lobbying under federal charity-tax rules. [California SOS definitions](https://www.sos.ca.gov/campaign-lobbying/cal-access-resources/cal-access-users-manual/glossary)

## Substantial-part test versus 501(h)

| Rule | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **Default substantial-part test** | The IRS considers facts and circumstances, including expenditures and compensated **and volunteer** time. The IRS does not provide a universal percentage safe harbor on this guidance page. [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/measuring-lobbying-substantial-part-test) |
| **501(h) expenditure election** | Eligible public charities file **Form 5768** during the tax year for which the election should apply. The election measures lobbying through defined expenditures and remains effective unless revoked. Churches and private foundations cannot use it. [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/measuring-lobbying-activity-expenditure-test) |

### 501(h) expenditure limits

Let **E** mean annual exempt-purpose expenditures—not donations received or the bank balance.

| E | Annual total lobbying nontaxable amount |
|---|---|
| Up to $500,000 | 20% of E |
| Over $500,000 through $1 million | $100,000 + 15% of the excess over $500,000 |
| Over $1 million through $1.5 million | $175,000 + 10% of the excess over $1 million |
| Over $1.5 million | $225,000 + 5% of the excess over $1.5 million, capped at $1 million |

These are the IRS’s statutory expenditure tiers. [IRS table](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/measuring-lobbying-activity-expenditure-test)

**Grassroots lobbying has a separate ceiling of 25% of the total lobbying allowance.** Thus, in the first tier, its ceiling is 5% of exempt-purpose expenditures. Excess expenditures can trigger excise tax, and repeated excessive lobbying can jeopardize exemption. [IRS Publication 557](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p557), [IRS expenditure-test guidance](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/measuring-lobbying-activity-expenditure-test)

## Education, direct lobbying, and grassroots lobbying

The following are applications of the IRS’s **501(h)** definitions:

| Proposed activity | Likely treatment |
|---|---|
| Publish a factual, sourced description of existing shutoff protections | Education, absent additional lobbying elements. |
| Teach how to identify representatives or submit comments, without promoting specific legislation | Generally education. |
| Tell a legislator to support a particular heat-protection bill or specific legislative proposal | Direct lobbying. |
| Tell website visitors to contact legislators to support that proposal | Grassroots lobbying. |
| Ask a utility commission to adopt protections under existing authority | Generally administrative advocacy for federal tax purposes. |

Direct lobbying generally requires communication with a legislator or relevant legislative participant, reference to specific legislation, and a view on it. Grassroots lobbying generally adds communication to the public and encouragement to act. **A proposal need not already have a bill number.** [IRS Schedule C definitions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i990sc), [Administrative advocacy](https://www.bolderadvocacy.org/resource/administrative-advocacy-/)

Calling material “educational” does not determine its treatment. The nonpartisan-analysis exception requires substantive standards; an action alert does not automatically qualify merely because it contains research. Preparation, distribution, and allocable overhead can count as lobbying expenditures. [IRS Schedule C instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i990sc)

**My recommendation:** track educational work, administrative advocacy, direct lobbying, and grassroots lobbying separately. Under 501(h), uncompensated volunteer effort generally does not consume the expenditure allowance, but organizational spending to recruit, train, or equip those volunteers can. [Bolder Advocacy’s explanation](https://bolderadvocacy.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/Worry_Free_Lobbying_paywall.pdf)

For a sponsored project, the sponsor must approve and administer lobbying within its own legal framework; do not assume the project has an independent 501(h) allowance. [Sponsor control](https://www.adlercolvin.com/blog/2012/10/15/what-is-fiscal-sponsorship/)

## California registration and reporting

California’s published definitions distinguish:

- **Contract lobbyists:** generally, individuals receiving or entitled to receive **$2,000 in a calendar month** for qualifying direct communications.
- **In-house lobbyists:** compensated employees spending **one-third or more of their time in a calendar month** in qualifying direct communications.
- **Lobbyist employers:** organizations employing qualifying lobbyists or retaining lobbying firms. [California SOS](https://www.sos.ca.gov/campaign-lobbying/cal-access-resources/cal-access-users-manual/glossary)

Separately, an organization without a lobbyist or lobbying firm may have to file **Form 645** when qualifying payments to influence legislative or administrative action total **$5,000 or more in a calendar quarter**. Therefore, “we only use volunteers” does not settle every disclosure obligation. [FPPC forms](https://www.fppc.ca.gov/forms)

California also has a specific utility-commission provision: certain communications at public hearings/workshops or included in the proceeding’s official record are excluded from the individual-lobbyist definition. Do not treat that as an exemption for every commission-related activity or payment. [Government Code §82039(c)](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-gov/title-9/chapter-2/section-82039/)

For other states, **there is no single nationwide state-registration threshold**; evaluate each jurisdiction’s definitions, compensation/spending triggers, administrative advocacy, and reporting rules. My recommendation is a state compliance review before authorizing each chapter’s advocacy. [Bolder Advocacy state-law resources](https://www.bolderadvocacy.org/resource-library/?pg=19)

## Would a 501(c)(4) be better?

A 501(c)(4) social-welfare organization may make lobbying germane to its mission its primary activity. Contributions generally are **not deductible as charitable donations**. New organizations generally must submit **Form 8976 within 60 days of formation**, with a **$50 fee**. [IRS social-welfare guidance](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/other-non-profits/social-welfare-organizations), [Deductibility](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p557), [Form 8976](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/electronically-submit-your-form-8976-notice-of-intent-to-operate-under-section-501c4)

**My recommendation:** consider a c4 if the actual operating plan is predominantly paid legislative campaigning. For a project centered on research, public education, commission participation, and bounded legislative lobbying, first explore a c3 host. A c3 cannot support or oppose candidates for elective office. [IRS campaign prohibition](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/restriction-of-political-campaign-intervention-by-section-501c3-tax-exempt-organizations)

# 4. Chapters: legal structure, name, and money

## Choose the structure before recruiting chapters

| Structure | Legal effect |
|---|---|
| **Internal branches of one parent** | Chapters are operating units with no separate legal existence. The parent oversees their activities and includes their finances in its reporting. There is no separate liability barrier between internal chapters and the parent. [Chapter-structure analysis](https://charitylawyerblog.com/2011/02/01/creating-nonprofit-chapters-and-affiliates/) |
| **Separate affiliates** | Chapters are separate legal entities linked by agreements. Their governance, exemption, banking, filings, and liability arrangements need separate attention; affiliation alone does not resolve these matters. [Legal framework](https://www.legalforgood.com/post/expansion-should-your-nonprofit-add-chapters-or-affiliates) |

**My recommendation:** start with internal student organizing teams approved by the host. A school club partnering with the project should have its relationship documented rather than simply declaring itself a legally covered chapter.

Operating or fundraising across state lines can create additional state registration obligations. Federal exemption is not a substitute for checking those obligations. [IRS state requirements](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/charitable-solicitation-state-requirements), [National Council of Nonprofits](https://www.councilofnonprofits.org/running-nonprofit/governance-leadership/state-filing-requirements-nonprofits)

## Group exemption: important 2026 update

[IRS Publication 4573](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p4573.pdf) explains the group-exemption concept, but its 2019 edition should be read alongside the **new Revenue Procedure 2026-8**, which supersedes the older procedure. The IRS resumed accepting group applications in 2026. [Current IRS procedure](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2026-04_IRB)

Current requirements include:

- A qualifying central organization with recognized exemption or an appropriate pending application.
- **At least five subordinate organizations** to obtain a new group exemption, and at least one to maintain it.
- Affiliation and central **general supervision or control**.
- Subordinates covered under the same paragraph of §501(c), with applicable uniform-purpose requirements.
- Separate subordinate identifying information and required authorizations. [IRS overview](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/group-exemption-rulings-and-group-returns)

Applications use **Form 8940 through Pay.gov**. Central organizations generally submit annual supplemental group information **30–90 days before their accounting year closes**. Annual return responsibilities remain; group exemption and a group return are different arrangements. [IRS application update](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USIRS/bulletins/40552f3), [Revenue Procedure 2026-8](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2026-04_IRB)

**My recommendation:** do not pursue group exemption for the initial internal chapter network. It is intended for separate subordinate organizations, not needed merely to give organizing teams a shared name. [IRS subordinate-organization framework](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/group-exemption-rulings-and-group-returns)

## Chapter agreements

A chapter agreement should establish permitted name use and the parties’ respective obligations. Financial standards, oversight, insurance, governance, and termination are recognized agreement topics. [Chapter-agreement guidance](https://study.nonprofitaccountingbasics.org/federal-tax-issues/eight-key-provisions-chapter-affiliation-agreement)

**My recommended provisions:**

- Approved mission, activities, territory, and adult contact.
- Clear internal-branch or separate-affiliate status.
- Authority to speak, lobby, fundraise, spend, and sign contracts.
- Financial reporting, receipts, restricted funds, and account access.
- Youth protection, insurance, privacy, and incident escalation.
- Name/logo license, quality standards, termination, and return of records/assets.
- Graduation and leadership-transition procedures.

## Trademark and name protection

A corporate-name filing or domain registration is not equivalent to trademark protection. Trademark rights may arise through use; federal registration provides broader protection and benefits. Search existing names and related marks before committing to a brand. [USPTO process](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/trademark-process), [Registration benefits](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/why-register-your-trademark)

The current federal **base application fee is $350 per class**, with possible additional application, intent-to-use, and maintenance fees. Unregistered services may use **SM**; **®** is reserved for registered marks. [USPTO costs](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/how-much-does-it-cost), [Symbols](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/what-trademark)

**My recommendation:** begin with a careful name search and written chapter-use rules. Defer federal filing until the name and organizational ownership are settled.

## Chapter money

**My recommended initial rule:** route donations through the sponsor/parent, record chapter budgets separately in its accounting, reimburse approved expenses against receipts, and require a second reviewer. Separate legal affiliates should use their own entity accounts and comply with their agreements. Written controls and separation of financial duties reduce opportunities for misuse. [Internal controls](https://www.councilofnonprofits.org/running-nonprofit/administration-and-financial-management/internal-controls-nonprofits), [Affiliate financial practices](https://www.yptc.com/best-practices-and-common-pitfalls-for-associations/)

# 6. Common mistakes and failure modes

Reliable student-nonprofit-specific failure-rate statistics were **not found**. The following are documented nonprofit risks applied to this founder’s situation.

| Mistake | Practical response |
|---|---|
| **Creating an entity before establishing a need or maintaining a program** | Test the work and investigate collaboration first. Nonprofit startup guidance emphasizes demonstrated need, financing, and measurable impact. [National Council of Nonprofits](https://www.councilofnonprofits.org/running-nonprofit/how-start-nonprofit/how-start-nonprofit-step-1-research) |
| **Confusing incorporation, EIN, and tax exemption** | Maintain separate checklists for SOS, IRS, FTB, and AG obligations. [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/federal-tax-obligations-of-nonprofit-corporations), [FTB](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/file/business/types/charities-nonprofits/help-with-charities-nonprofits.html) |
| **Missing annual filings after graduation or inactivity** | Assign an adult compliance owner and preserve confirmation receipts. Failure to file required IRS returns/notices for three consecutive years generally causes automatic revocation. [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/obtaining-an-employer-identification-number-for-an-exempt-organization) |
| **Treating a sponsor as someone lending an EIN** | Use a real agreement and accept sponsor oversight. The sponsor must retain discretion and control over charitable funds. [Adler & Colvin](https://www.adlercolvin.com/blog/2012/10/15/what-is-fiscal-sponsorship/) |
| **Calling every advocacy activity “education”** | Classify the actual communication and spending; distinguish commission action from legislation. [IRS definitions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i990sc) |
| **Launching chapters without control over money, safety, or name use** | Approve a chapter structure and written agreement before recruitment. [Chapter-agreement guidance](https://study.nonprofitaccountingbasics.org/federal-tax-issues/eight-key-provisions-chapter-affiliation-agreement) |
| **Concentrating money and account access in one student** | Use organizational accounts, receipts, second-person review, and shared institutional access. [Internal controls](https://www.councilofnonprofits.org/running-nonprofit/administration-and-financial-management/internal-controls-nonprofits) |
| **Failing to plan for the founder’s departure** | Document handoffs and train successors. Leadership-transition planning protects organizational continuity. [Succession guidance](https://www.councilofnonprofits.org/running-nonprofit/governance-leadership/succession-planning-nonprofits-managing-leadership) |
| **Publishing an oversimplified or stale heat-protection map** | My recommendation: record legal authority, covered utilities/customers, trigger, exceptions, effective dates, and last verification. Rules can change: CPUC reported a temporary summer 2026 change to its temperature threshold. [CPUC update](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/news-and-updates/all-news/how-long-term-energy-planning-is-benefitting-california-in-summer) |

For the website, I recommend distinguishing **nonpayment disconnections** from safety shutoffs and grid outages, and using **“not found”** when research is incomplete rather than labeling a state “no protection.” Those are different service-interruption circumstances. [CPUC utility complaints](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/consumer-support/file-a-complaint/utility-complaint/cab-faqs), [CPUC grid-outage explanation](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/industries-and-topics/electrical-energy/flex-alert)

# Recommended path for this founder

**This is my recommendation: begin as a student-led project under an approved school/nonprofit host or a suitable Model A fiscal sponsor. Reconsider independent incorporation after demonstrating that the project can be maintained.** Fiscal sponsorship is an established alternative to immediately forming a separate charity. [Public Counsel](https://publiccounsel.org/publications/fiscal-sponsorship-an-alternative-to-forming-a-nonprofit-501c3-corporation/)

1. **Approach HCB first**, describing all three activities honestly: policy publishing, legislative/commission advocacy training, and interstate student chapters. Its published age criteria fit this founder, but acceptance of this advocacy plan remains unverified. Obtain that approval in writing. [HCB eligibility](https://help.hcb.hackclub.com/en/articles/15409923-who-can-apply-for-fiscal-sponsorship)

2. **Build a small, reviewed policy database before expanding chapters.** I recommend primary-source links, quoted legal language where necessary, verification dates, and a corrections process for every entry.

3. **Use a student leadership team with accountable adult support.** Give adults defined responsibility for contracts, money, compliance, and youth protection while students direct research and organizing.

4. **Keep initial chapters as approved internal teams**, with centralized funds and a short written agreement. Avoid separate corporations or group exemption at the pilot stage. [Chapter structures](https://charitylawyerblog.com/2011/02/01/creating-nonprofit-chapters-and-affiliates/)

5. **Choose the tax structure around the real advocacy plan.** For an independent eligible c3, I would normally explore the 501(h) election. If paid legislative campaigning becomes the main program, examine a c4 before committing to c3 restrictions. [IRS 501(h)](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/measuring-lobbying-activity-expenditure-test), [IRS c4](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/other-non-profits/social-welfare-organizations)
