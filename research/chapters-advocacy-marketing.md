## Citizens’ Climate Lobby: supported local chapters, with separate youth teams

- **Starting a chapter:** CCL provides group-start workshops, leadership training, and hands-on support; each chapter has a group leader. Its college chapter guide directs organizers to have a state leader or network director submit a group-update form for official recognition. A universal U.S. minimum membership requirement was **not found**. [CCL resource directory](https://community.citizensclimate.org/resources/item/19/261)
- **Accountability:** Group leaders receive new-member information and action reports covering activities, endorsements, and published media. These reports help chapters assess progress and follow up with recruits. A public U.S. chapter affiliation contract or automatic inactivity threshold was **not found**. [Group leader reports](https://community.citizensclimate.org/resources/item/19/403)
- **High school versus college/community:** Youth can join a local multigenerational chapter or organize local/state youth teams with support from CCL’s Youth Team Network. [CCL youth participation](https://youth.citizensclimatelobby.org/get-involved/), [college chapter development](https://citizensclimatehighered.org/chapter-development)
- **Operating philosophy:** CCL explicitly emphasizes respectful, nonpartisan relationships with officials and community leaders. [About CCL](https://citizensclimatelobby.org/about-ccl/)

**Useful feature:** A small central team can provide researched materials and coaching while volunteers build relationships locally. That is an adaptation of CCL’s documented support structure, not a requirement to reproduce its scale. [Volunteer support](https://citizensclimateeducation.org/programs-and-tools/empowering-a-national-volunteer-corps/)

## Sunrise Movement: distinguish a small student hub from a developed chapter

- **Starting:** Sunrise directs prospective organizers to its New Hubs program. Its current directory distinguishes **student hubs**, typically with at least **five active members at the same educational institution**; **community hubs**, typically with **5–30 active members**; and **chapters**, community hubs with at least **30 active members** that meet additional criteria. The word “typically” matters: the page does not present every number as a universal application cutoff. [Hub models](https://www.sunrisemovement.org/hubs/), [New Hubs entry point](https://action.sunrisemovement.org/a/newhubs)
- **National support:** Sunrise offers a member portal containing resources, support offerings, an internal calendar, and organizing information; its public website also links a style guide and student organizing materials. [Take action and resources](https://www.sunrisemovement.org/take-action/), [style-guide entry point](https://www.sunrisemovement.org/hubs/)
- **Rules and autonomy:** Sunrise NYC’s published principles allow a group of three to take action under Sunrise’s name. That describes decentralized action, **not the current national definition of a student hub**. [NYC principles](https://www.sunrise-nyc.org/principles), [current national hub categories](https://www.sunrisemovement.org/hubs/)
- **Accountability:** The national site directs existing hubs to update their information through HubHub. The complete current hub agreement, reporting requirements, removal procedure, and high-school adviser requirement were **not found in accessible public materials**. [Hub directory and update process](https://www.sunrisemovement.org/hubs/)

**A particularly relevant historical model:** Sunrise’s Strike Circle program supplied **six meeting agendas, short videos, and a coach**. Organizers reported launching **600 small groups** and converting **more than 300** into ongoing chapters. These are historical program figures, not current chapter counts or a general retention rate. [Firsthand program retrospective by Sunrise organizer Luis Marchese and PowerLabs](https://commonslibrary.org/sunrise-strike-circle/)

### “Perceptions of Citizen Advocacy on Capitol Hill”

This separate survey ran **October 12–December 13, 2010**, with **260 respondents**. CMF reported “some” or “a lot” of influence for Washington constituent visits (**97%**), district/state-office constituent visits (**94%**), town-hall questions (**87%**), and letters to the editor (**80%**). ## State legislative meetings and lobby days

**Everytown’s documented sequence** is: gather volunteers, provide a short training, review legislative priorities and specific asks, then divide into meetings—preferably constituents meeting their own district lawmakers. It also coordinates remote supporters through calls, email, and social posts. [Advocacy-day explanation](https://www.everytown.org/what-are-moms-demand-action-advocacy-days/)

For the practical paperwork, the American College of Surgeons publishes a state advocacy-day toolkit with a project checklist, schedule template, legislative appointment guidance, meeting-report worksheet, and evaluation form. These are useful operational examples even though ACS is not a youth organization. [State advocacy-day toolkit](https://www.facs.org/advocacy/state-legislation/state-advocacy-toolkit/)

**Suggested small-project adaptation:**

1. Identify the state legislator, relevant committee, and precise policy change.
2. Request a meeting as a constituent; identify the students who will attend.
3. Bring a short state-specific brief and a concrete request.
4. Record the response, promised follow-up, and next decision date.
5. Send the requested information and maintain the relationship.

This proposed workflow adapts Everytown’s constituent matching and CCL’s meeting preparation/follow-up practices. [Everytown](https://www.everytown.org/what-are-moms-demand-action-advocacy-days/), [CCL meeting roles](https://citizensclimatelobby.org/blog/advocacy/how-to-meet-with-your-member-of-congress/)

## Testifying at state legislative committee hearings

Washington provides an especially clear official example:

- Anyone may testify; participants can submit written testimony, register a position, or testify in person/remotely.
- Sign-up opens when a bill is placed on a public-hearing agenda and closes **one hour before** the meeting.
- Written testimony can be submitted online until **24 hours after the meeting starts**.
- Speakers should identify themselves, say whom they represent, state their position, avoid repeating others, and offer a later written answer when uncertain.

These are **Washington procedures**, not universal national deadlines. [Washington Legislature testimony guide](https://leg.wa.gov/bills-meetings-and-session/session/how-to-testify-at-a-committee-meeting/)

**Suggested testimony outline:** “My name is __; I live in __; I represent __/myself; I support __; here is the gap in current protection; here is the requested amendment.” This adapts the legislature’s own guidance on identification, representation, brevity, and explaining support. [Official testimony guidance](https://leg.wa.gov/bills-meetings-and-session/session/how-to-testify-at-a-committee-meeting/)

## Filing a public comment with a state utility commission

### California CPUC: ordinary public comments

The CPUC’s published process is:

1. Search the **Docket Card** by proceeding number or keyword.
2. Select the appropriate proceeding.
3. Open **Public Comments**.
4. Select **Add Public Comment**.
5. Complete the required fields and submit.

The CPUC requires name, city, state, ZIP code, and email. It says identifying information other than email is publicly displayed unless the commenter selects the private-comment option. The guide should therefore explain the privacy choice before a student submits. [CPUC comment instructions](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/about-cpuc/divisions/news-and-public-information-office/public-advisors-office/providing-public-comments-at-the-cpuc)

**Public participation and formal party participation are different:** the CPUC explicitly says parties must still formally file their comments. Its participation materials separately explain requesting party status and the accompanying responsibilities. [Public-comment distinction](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/about-cpuc/divisions/news-and-public-information-office/public-advisors-office/providing-public-comments-at-the-cpuc), [participation resources](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/about-cpuc/divisions/news-and-public-information-office/public-advisors-office/providing-public-comments-at-the-cpuc/brochures-on-cpuc-processes)

The CPUC also holds free public participation hearings. No RSVP is required, but attendees wishing to speak must sign up; the administrative law judge may impose time limits. Language/ASL interpretation can be requested through the Public Advisor’s Office, generally at least **three business days** ahead. [Public participation hearings](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/proceedings-and-rulemaking/cpuc-public-participation-hearings/)

### New York PSC: another usable docket model

New York’s Department of Public Service instructs people to find the case in its Document and Matter Management system, select **Post Comments**, supply name/address/email, and optionally attach a document. It separately explains subscribing for case updates. [New York comment and subscription instructions](https://dps.ny.gov/node/8236)

### What a project-specific commission comment should contain

**Suggested structure:** proceeding number; utility/service territory; the protection currently missing; a sourced local example or clearly labeled analysis; the precise rule requested; and why the request fits that proceeding. This is an adaptation of the agencies’ case-specific comment systems and RESULTS’ problem/solution/action framework. [CPUC instructions](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/about-cpuc/divisions/news-and-public-information-office/public-advisors-office/providing-public-comments-at-the-cpuc), [New York instructions](https://dps.ny.gov/node/8236), [RESULTS framework](https://results.org/resources/create-and-deliver-a-laser-talk)

**Do not automatically use legislative lobbying tactics with commissioners:** CPUC rules vary by proceeding category. Ex parte communications are prohibited in adjudicatory proceedings, restricted/reportable in ratesetting proceedings, and generally permitted in quasi-legislative proceedings; special scoping instructions can matter. [CPUC ex parte guidance](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/about-cpuc/transparency-and-reporting/ex-parte-communications)

## RESULTS’ “laser talk” and CCL’s meeting structure

### RESULTS: EPIC

RESULTS teaches talks lasting **less than two minutes**:

- **E — Engage:** a short hook or personal statement.
- **P — Problem:** explain the problem and its consequences.
- **I — Inform:** present the solution.
- **C — Call to action:** make a specific request that can be followed up.

RESULTS recommends speaking naturally, practicing aloud, and rehearsing with feedback. [Laser-talk instructions](https://results.org/resources/create-and-deliver-a-laser-talk)

**Draft adaptation—not a factual claim about any particular state:**

> I’m a student from [city] researching electricity shutoff protections during dangerous heat. Our review identifies [precisely sourced gap] in [state’s rule]. We are asking for [specific protection], using [identified state] as a comparison. Will you [sponsor the bill/request a commission review/support the amendment]? When should we follow up?

### CCL: assign responsibilities, then listen

CCL’s meeting guidance identifies roles including leader, appreciator, timekeeper, note-taker, asker, and follow-up. The leader assigns roles and organizes practice; introductions establish relevant personal connections; the note-taker prioritizes what officials say; the asker presents the legislative request; and someone sends promised materials afterward. [CCL meeting roles](https://citizensclimatelobby.org/blog/advocacy/how-to-meet-with-your-member-of-congress/)

**Suggested adaptation:** A small team can share these responsibilities. The useful feature is clear ownership of the ask, notes, and follow-up—not appointing a large delegation. [Underlying meeting structure](https://citizensclimatelobby.org/blog/advocacy/how-to-meet-with-your-member-of-congress/)

## Legal and school-club notes

### Can minors lobby?

**Minors can participate in ordinary issue advocacy; voting eligibility is not the prerequisite for expressing political views or petitioning government.** First Amendment protections extend to students, and Washington’s legislature explicitly welcomes anyone to testify. This should not be confused with a blanket exemption from lobbying disclosure laws. [Student speech rights](https://constitutioncenter.org/the-constitution/supreme-court-case-library/tinker-v-des-moines-independent-community-school-district%26gt), [petition rights](https://constitutioncenter.org/education/classroom-resources-by-topic/first-amendment-assembly-and-petition-clauses), [public testimony](https://leg.wa.gov/bills-meetings-and-session/session/how-to-testify-at-a-committee-meeting/)

California’s statutory definition of lobbyist generally concerns compensated communications or relevant employee duties. Separately, an organization can become a **$5,000 filer** by spending at least **$5,000 in a calendar quarter** to influence legislative/administrative action without hiring a lobbyist. Thus, “volunteer” and “small” should not be turned into an unconditional national legal exemption. [California statutory definition](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/code-gov/title-9/chapter-2/section-82039/), [FPPC filing categories](https://www.fppc.ca.gov/learn/lobbying-rules/)

### 501(c)(3) lobbying and the 501(h) election

- A 501(c)(3) organization **may lobby**, but lobbying cannot constitute a substantial part of its activities under the default test. The IRS includes state legislation in “legislation”; executive, judicial, and administrative actions are excluded from that particular definition. State lobbying-disclosure laws remain a separate question. [IRS lobbying guidance](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/lobbying)
- Eligible public charities can elect the expenditure test using **Form 5768**. For organizations with no more than **$500,000 in exempt-purpose expenditures**, the lobbying nontaxable amount is **20%** of those expenditures; the scale decreases for larger organizations and has an overall **$1 million** cap. [IRS expenditure test](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/measuring-lobbying-activity-expenditure-test)
- The grassroots-lobbying nontaxable amount is **25% of the total lobbying nontaxable amount**—not an additional unrestricted allowance. [IRS Publication 557](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p557)
- Candidate campaign intervention is a different category: the IRS prohibits 501(c)(3) organizations from supporting or opposing candidates for elective office. [IRS campaign-intervention guidance](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/restriction-of-political-campaign-intervention-by-section-501c3-tax-exempt-organizations)

**Implication for this project:** These provisions describe charitable organizations’ tax treatment. They are not a reason to incorporate simply to publish research or teach advocacy. The National Council of Nonprofits explicitly encourages considering alternatives before forming an organization. [Alternatives and startup considerations](https://www.councilofnonprofits.org/running-nonprofit/how-start-nonprofit)

### Fiscal sponsorship

A fiscal sponsor can receive charitable contributions and provide administrative/financial oversight for a project without requiring the project to obtain its own exemption. The sponsor must retain discretion and control over donated funds; it is not simply lending out an EIN. Written agreements should allocate responsibilities, fees, reporting, and donation compliance. [National Council of Nonprofits](https://www.councilofnonprofits.org/running-nonprofit/administration-and-financial-management/fiscal-sponsorship-nonprofits)

Some projects remain sponsored long term. For this student project, sponsorship becomes relevant if a concrete grant, tax-deductible fundraising need, or administrative requirement arises—not because chapters sound more official with nonprofit status. The latter is my recommendation, informed by the sponsorship alternatives described here. [Sponsorship options](https://www.councilofnonprofits.org/running-nonprofit/administration-and-financial-management/fiscal-sponsorship-nonprofits)

### School clubs

The federal Equal Access Act applies to federally funded public secondary schools with a limited open forum. It prohibits discrimination against student meetings based on political, religious, philosophical, or other content. It also requires voluntary, student-initiated meetings, protects school order, and restricts nonschool persons from directing or regularly attending group activities. [20 U.S.C. §4071](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/4071)

**Practical recommendation:** Before establishing a school chapter, check that school’s recognition, adviser, room-use, fundraising, trip, and publicity procedures. Those requirements cannot be determined nationally from the organization’s registration form alone. CCL and SDA explicitly distinguish organizational onboarding from school recognition. [CCL campus guidance](https://citizensclimatehighered.org/chapter-development), [SDA high-school toolkit](https://studentsdemandaction.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/10/2024/08/SDA-HighSchool-Organizing-Toolkit.pdf)

# 4. Campaigns that won utility shutoff protections

## Arizona, 2019: reporting and consumer pressure followed by emergency rulemaking

**Correction:** The woman central to this controversy was **Stephanie Pullman**, not Stephanie Pratt. Pullman died in 2018 after APS disconnected her electricity; the case became a major public issue in June 2019. [Contemporaneous reporting](https://www.kjzz.org/2019-06-21/content-1018581-stacey-champion-arizona-corporation-commission-slow-act-after-heat-related-death)

**Who was involved:** Reporting exposed the death and disconnection practices. Consumer advocate Stacey Champion had organized a petition challenging APS rates and crowdfunded a lawyer for that challenge. Her broader rate campaign should not be described as a separately proven sole cause of the shutoff rule. [KJZZ account](https://www.kjzz.org/2019-06-21/content-1018581-stacey-champion-arizona-corporation-commission-slow-act-after-heat-related-death)

**Timeline and result:**

- **June 13, 2019:** A commission docket opened to investigate disconnection rules and utility policies.
- **June 14:** The chair requested emergency rulemaking.
- **June 20:** The commission adopted emergency amendments.
  
These steps are recorded in Decision **77260**, docket **RU-00000A-19-0132**. [Commission order](https://apps.azsos.gov/public_services/register/2019/Notice_Archive/R19-129.pdf)

The emergency rule prohibited covered residential disconnections **June 1–October 15** and included additional payment-related protections. Contemporary reporting described the measure as temporary, effective for **180 days**. [Emergency rule text](https://apps.azsos.gov/public_services/register/2019/28/contents.pdf), [June 20 reporting](https://www.phoenixnewtimes.com/news/customer-death-sparks-ban-on-summer-power-shutoffs-in-arizona-11316166/)

**Documented tactics:** Investigative reporting, public consumer advocacy, a rate petition/legal challenge, and commission emergency rulemaking. A quantified account of how many constituent contacts changed commissioners’ positions was **not found**.

**Transferable lesson—an inference:** A commission can act on an identified safety gap without waiting for a new statute; historical emergency rules must still be tracked through subsequent permanent proceedings.

**Current-status caution:** Arizona’s rules subsequently evolved. An April 2026 attorney-general announcement described a proposed APS settlement adding a temperature hold outside the calendar moratorium and explicitly said the judgment was not final until signed. Do not treat that announcement alone as proof of final court approval or a rule covering every Arizona utility. [Arizona AG announcement](https://www.azag.gov/press-release/attorney-general-mayes-secures-7-million-settlement-aps-following-investigation)

## Illinois, 2023–2024: an attorney-general-backed statutory improvement

**Who led it:** Attorney General Kwame Raoul initiated **HB1541**, sponsored by **Sen. Mattie Hunter** and **Rep. Eva-Dina Delgado**. His office argued that the previous threshold missed dangerous heat involving humidity. [May 4, 2023 announcement](https://illinoisattorneygeneral.gov/news/story/attorney-general-raoul-applauds-passage-of-bill-to-protect-consumers-from-utility-disconnections)

**Timeline:** The measure passed the legislature in 2023, was approved on **June 9, 2023**, became **Public Act 103-0019**, and took effect **January 1, 2024**. Thus, this particular win is best dated **2023 enactment/2024 implementation**, rather than described as a new 2025 heat law. [Official bill history](https://www.ilga.gov/ftp/legislation/103/BillStatus/HTML/10300HB1541.html), [Public Act](https://www.ilga.gov/Legislation/publicacts/view/103-0019)

**What changed:** For covered residential cooling service, the law lowered the forecast-temperature trigger from **95°F to 90°F**, added National Weather Service excessive-heat watches/advisories/warnings, and addressed forecasts preceding holidays/weekends. The enacted text also removed the previous **over-100,000-residential-customers** limitation in the cooling provision. [Enacted text](https://www.ilga.gov/Legislation/publicacts/view/103-0019)

**Documented tactics:** The AG’s office used a public-health argument, utility disconnection compliance data, legislative sponsors, bipartisan passage, and subsequent consumer education about the new protection. [Legislative rationale](https://illinoisattorneygeneral.gov/news/story/attorney-general-raoul-applauds-passage-of-bill-to-protect-consumers-from-utility-disconnections), [2024 implementation reminder](https://illinoisattorneygeneral.gov/news/story/attorney-general-raoul-reminds-illinois-residents-they-are-protected-from-utility-disconnections-during-extreme-heat)

A separately documented youth-led campaign or named grassroots coalition responsible for HB1541 was **not found**.

**Transferable lesson—an inference:** Show the concrete gap in an existing rule, identify a government office with consumer-protection expertise, and seek a legislator willing to carry the amendment.

## California, 2024–2026: sustained intervention to strengthen an existing protection

**Who campaigned:** TURN sought stronger extreme-heat protections. The Center for Accessible Technology and National Consumer Law Center supported its motion, as did the CPUC’s Public Advocates Office. TURN and the Center for Biological Diversity publicly announced the eventual improvement. [Joint consumer-advocate filing](https://docs.cpuc.ca.gov/PublishedDocs/Efile/G000/M607/K785/607785658.PDF), [campaign announcement](https://biologicaldiversity.org/w/news/press-releases/california-strengthens-protections-against-disconnections-during-extreme-heat-2026-07-16/)

**Timeline:**

- **August 14, 2024:** TURN filed an emergency motion seeking revision of the existing extreme-heat trigger. Its argument included risks below the existing **100°F** threshold and differences in local acclimatization and nighttime heat. [Subsequent filing documenting the motion](https://docs.cpuc.ca.gov/PublishedDocs/Efile/G000/M607/K785/607785658.PDF)
- **June 2025:** The commission directed utilities to propose stronger, data-informed protections addressing relative heat exposure. [Campaign account](https://biologicaldiversity.org/w/news/press-releases/california-strengthens-protections-against-disconnections-during-extreme-heat-2026-07-16/)
- **July 16, 2026:** The CPUC lowered the threshold from **100°F to 90°F**. Its own explanation describes the adjustment as applying that summer while broader disconnection actions remained under consideration. [CPUC confirmation](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/news-and-updates/all-news/how-long-term-energy-planning-is-benefitting-california-in-summer)

The campaign announcement also reports a requirement involving **CalHeatScore**, and describes utilities’ unsuccessful proposal to retain the 100°F threshold. [Joint announcement](https://biologicaldiversity.org/w/news/press-releases/california-strengthens-protections-against-disconnections-during-extreme-heat-2026-07-16/)

**Documented tactics:** A formal emergency motion, supporting filings by consumer/disability advocates, public-health evidence, scrutiny of utility proposals, and coalition communications. Evidence assigning a particular share of the outcome to ordinary public comments or student organizing was **not found**. [Consumer-advocate filing](https://docs.cpuc.ca.gov/PublishedDocs/Efile/G000/M607/K785/607785658.PDF), [campaign announcement](https://biologicaldiversity.org/w/news/press-releases/california-strengthens-protections-against-disconnections-during-extreme-heat-2026-07-16/)

**Transferable lesson—an inference:** A student project can contribute clear research and constituent participation while experienced intervenors handle motions and technical filings. The website should preserve the distinction between interim and permanent protections.

## New York, 2024–2026: standardizing protections through a commission proceeding

**The proceeding:** Case **24-M-0586** addresses establishment of extreme-heat protections, practices, and procedures. [Official docket](https://documents.dps.ny.gov/public/MatterManagement/CaseMaster.aspx?MatterSeq=74623)

**Who participated:** The commission’s order identifies comments from PULP, New York City, utilities, Columbia University, Waterfront Alliance, and joint commenters including **WE ACT for Environmental Justice, Earthjustice, NRDC, Local Nature Lab, and Waterfront Alliance**. [Order identifying commenters](https://documents.dps.ny.gov/public/Common/ViewDoc.aspx?DocRefId=%7B80AD069D-0000-C1AE-9DD8-F2A34BD05511%7D&DocTitle=Order+Adopting+Utility+Customer+Protections+During+Periods+of+Extreme+Heat+and+Directing+Utility+Filings)

**Timeline:** PULP filed comments on the staff recommendations on **August 4, 2025**. The PSC approved uniform protections on **March 19, 2026**. [PULP filing](https://documents.dps.ny.gov/public/Common/ViewDoc.aspx?DocRefId=%7BC0627698-0000-CB6A-9DCA-19208618AC26%7D), [PSC announcement](https://dps.ny.gov/node/28806)

**What was won:** The policy covers major investor-owned electric utilities and covered water utilities; prohibits residential nonpayment terminations when forecast or actual **heat index reaches 90°F**; and adds protection for **the following two days in identified heat islands**. The PSC also required standardized tariff language and customer communications. It suggested—not ordered in the same terms—that LIPA consider consistent protections. [PSC description of coverage and requirements](https://dps.ny.gov/node/28806)

**Documented tactics:** Participation in the staff-proposal/comment process, coordinated filings, health/heat-island arguments, and work across utility rate proceedings. The PSC described years of work by staff, intervenors, and utilities to improve and standardize protections. [Order](https://documents.dps.ny.gov/public/Common/ViewDoc.aspx?DocRefId=%7B80AD069D-0000-C1AE-9DD8-F2A34BD05511%7D&DocTitle=Order+Adopting+Utility+Customer+Protections+During+Periods+of+Extreme+Heat+and+Directing+Utility+Filings), [PSC background](https://dps.ny.gov/node/28806)

**Do not conflate this with a bill:** S120’s legislative page separately records passage through both chambers; that status is not itself proof of gubernatorial enactment. The March 2026 commission protection is the verified regulatory outcome described above. [Separate bill record](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S120/amendment/A)

**Transferable lesson—an inference:** Effective advocacy can ask a commission to replace inconsistent utility-specific practices with a common standard, while separately tracking any legislative proposal.

# Directly usable for this project

The following are **proposed adaptations**, not claims that the project has already implemented them.

- **Keep the current structure until a concrete need arises.** Continue as a small student-run project; investigate fiscal sponsorship if a grant, donation, or administrative requirement makes it useful. [Alternatives to incorporation](https://www.councilofnonprofits.org/running-nonprofit/how-start-nonprofit)
- **Pilot local organizing before announcing a network.** Give interested students a researched state brief, first-meeting agenda, practice exercise, and one real action. Sunrise’s supported-circle approach is a useful precedent. [Program model](https://commonslibrary.org/sunrise-strike-circle/)
- **Use a short affiliation document.** Specify the mission, approved name, accurate representation of participation, who may speak for the project, account ownership/access, conduct expectations, and how affiliation ends. MFOL provides a concrete—much more elaborate—example. [Agreement](https://secure.everyaction.com/54sJhYAiX0iEbjgsCoPgAw2)
- **Count people honestly.** Separately report active working volunteers, local leaders, subscribers, and event participants. Use activity records rather than treating an interest-form submission as an active chapter. [Examples of activity reporting](https://results.org/volunteers/reporting-your-advocacy-actions), [monthly club reporting](https://girlup.org/girlup101)
- **Make each state page lead to the correct decision-maker.** Include the legislature route and commission route, relevant bill/docket, submission instructions, and the specific action requested. [Legislative participation example](https://leg.wa.gov/bills-meetings-and-session/session/how-to-testify-at-a-committee-meeting/), [commission participation example](https://dps.ny.gov/node/8236)
- **Publish the conditions behind “protected.”** Record utility coverage, customer eligibility, temperature versus heat index, forecast window, calendar dates, alert triggers, effective dates, and interim/permanent status. Illinois, California, and New York illustrate why these distinctions change the answer. [Illinois](https://www.ilga.gov/Legislation/publicacts/view/103-0019), [California](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/news-and-updates/all-news/how-long-term-energy-planning-is-benefitting-california-in-summer), [New York](https://dps.ny.gov/node/28806)
- **Define the heat-day calculation explicitly.** Distinguish a rule’s theoretical coverage from observed enforcement, and state whether the calculation uses actual weather or the forecast specified in the rule. These are methodological recommendations prompted by the differing legal triggers. [Illinois forecast trigger](https://www.ilga.gov/Legislation/publicacts/view/103-0019), [New York actual/forecast heat-index trigger](https://dps.ny.gov/node/28806)
- **Give volunteers adaptable language.** Provide an EPIC script, a meeting request, a testimony outline, and a commission-comment outline; require users to supply their real location and experience. [RESULTS structure](https://results.org/resources/create-and-deliver-a-laser-talk)
- **Build follow-up into the guide.** Record the official’s answer, promised material, responsible volunteer, and next date. [CCL meeting responsibilities](https://citizensclimatelobby.org/blog/advocacy/how-to-meet-with-your-member-of-congress/)
- **Start growth with useful content.** A state comparison, a short submission walkthrough, and an account of a real meeting are practical initial formats. SDA documents behind-the-scenes advocacy videos and accessible explainers. [Content examples](https://studentsdemandaction.org/social-media-advocacy-creating-change/)
- **Plan for school transitions.** Keep a current local contact, shared project records, a successor, and a simple active/inactive designation. This is a proposed response to documented reactivation needs, not a claim about why particular chapters lapsed. [Reactivation examples](https://studentsdemandaction.org/gun-safety-movement-2025-wrapped/)
- **Protect students’ information and stories.** Explain public-comment disclosure before submission; obtain appropriate permission before publishing personal accounts or images. [CPUC disclosure rules](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/about-cpuc/divisions/news-and-public-information-office/public-advisors-office/providing-public-comments-at-the-cpuc), [youth-consent policy example](https://media-girlup-org.s3.amazonaws.com/2020/06/2020-Girl-Up-Safety-Security-Policy.pdf)

Not checked: private chapter manuals, a fresh 49-jurisdiction audit, or every underlying docket; some PDF details rely on indexed excerpts because downloading for MarkItDown was blocked.  
Main risk: older manuals and changing/interim utility rules must be rechecked before publishing requirements or current protection counts.
