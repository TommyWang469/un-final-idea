What we stand for

No household should lose its electricity for an unpaid bill on a day the heat is dangerous.

What we do

We measure whether state rules protect people on the days the National Weather Service calls the heat dangerous. We publish each state's rule with its source, so anyone can check our work, and we help people bring those facts to the officials who set the rules.

What we advocate

A rule in every state

States with no hot-weather shut-off rule should adopt one.

Triggers that match the danger

A rule should be in force on the days the weather service warns of dangerous heat. Rules tied to heat advisories come closest to this.

Protection for every household

A rule should cover all residential customers, not only some groups.

Who we work with

Shut-off rules are mostly set by states, so our work focuses on state legislators and state utility commissions.

Why it matters globally

Our work supports UN Sustainable Development Goal 7, target 7.1: universal access to affordable, reliable energy. It also bears on target 13.1, resilience to climate hazards, and on Goal 3, good health.

What comes next

We are building an advocacy guide that shows people how to contact their lawmakers, then local chapters in other states.
