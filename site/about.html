About us

No one should lose power for an unpaid bill on a dangerously hot day

We're a student-led project. We check whether each state's rules protect people on the days the heat is dangerous, publish every rule with its source, and help people bring those facts to the officials who can change them.

What we ask for

A rule in every state

Half the states have no rule that stops heat-wave shut-offs. Every state should have one.

A trigger that matches the danger

A rule should be on whenever the weather service warns of dangerous heat. Rules tied to those heat alerts come closest. Rules set at a high fixed temperature can miss most dangerous days.

Protection for every household

A rule should cover every home, not only some groups.

Why this matters beyond the US

Our work supports the UN's Sustainable Development Goal 7, target 7.1: affordable, reliable energy for everyone. It also bears on target 13.1, staying safe from climate hazards, and on Goal 3, good health. The method travels too: take any rule meant to protect people from a climate hazard, and test whether it's on when the hazard actually hits.

Team

    Start a chapter in your state

    A chapter is a small group of students who take their state's numbers to their own lawmakers and utility commission. We don't have any chapters yet. We'd like the first ones to be in states where a fight is live right now: Massachusetts, Pennsylvania and Florida. Any state without a rule is welcome.

    What you get

    • A one-page brief on your state: its rule, its numbers, and its sources.
    • An agenda for your first meeting.
    • One real action to take together, like a meeting request or a comment to the utility commission.
    • The guide, scripts and templates on our Take action page.

    What we ask

    • A short, one-page agreement. It covers how to use the name, who can speak for the project, and how a chapter ends.
    • An adult adviser, and a parent's OK for every member under 18.
    • A short monthly update on what you did. A chapter that misses two in a row is listed as inactive.
    • Honest numbers. Count active members, not sign-ups. Cite only our published figures as ours.
    • No candidates. Chapters never support or oppose anyone running for office.
    • A successor. Before you graduate, name the next lead and hand over your records.

    Interested? Leave us a note on GitHub with your state and school.

    Others working on this

    Experts have studied heat shut-offs for years. We learn from them, and we aren't affiliated with any of them. What the issue lacks is volunteers in the states with no rule. That's the gap we're trying to fill.

    • Energy Justice Lab (Indiana University) tracks shut-offs and state rules, and publishes research.
    • National Consumer Law Center writes model rules and recommends heat-index or heat-alert triggers.
    • Energy and Policy Institute watches utilities and analyzed the federal shut-off data.
    • TURN and the Center for Biological Diversity led the push for California's 90°F rule.
    • RMI wrote a handbook on shut-offs for regulators.
    • NEADA, the group of state energy-assistance directors, works on funding for bill help.
    • Network for Public Health Law published a 2026 fact sheet on state heat shut-off laws.
