When it's dangerously hot, can your power be cut off?

In 24 of 48 states and DC, no state rule stops it.

How to read this

Two numbers for every state

Heat-alert days are the days the National Weather Service warned that the heat was dangerous in the state's five biggest counties. We use the yearly average from 2015 to 2024.

Uncovered days are the alert days when the state's rule, as written today, would not have stopped a utility from cutting off a home's power for an unpaid bill.

A rule that turns on with a weather-service alert covers every alert day by design. So a zero there doesn't prove the rule is well set. The test tells you most about rules set at a fixed temperature. How we counted
