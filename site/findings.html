What the check found

On the days the National Weather Service called the heat dangerous, was a household protected?

Without a rule, shut-offs continue through summer

A household that can't pay can lose electricity, and with it the air conditioning or fan. Some states bar this in extreme heat. Most coverage stops at counting those states. This site tests each rule against the days the heat was dangerous.

13.4 millionelectric disconnections in the US in 2024. One home can be counted more than once.
1,412,514of those fell in June to August in the 24 states that have no hot-weather rule today.
3,499June to August shut-offs in Arizona, which has a summer rule, against 29,023 in December alone.

National shut-offs do not peak in summer: July 2024 was the lowest month, with 967,012. Nobody counts how many people are harmed by heat-wave shut-offs. People have died, though. Source for all counts: EIA 2024 Residential Utility Disconnections Report.

A $51 balance on a 107°F day

In September 2018 an Arizona utility cut power to a 72-year-old woman. The medical examiner found environmental heat exposure combined with heart disease. Associated Press

A rule on paper, dropped three days earlier

In May 2024 the same utility disconnected an 82-year-old customer on a 99°F day, three days after dropping its temperature policy. She was found dead six days later. The utility agreed to a $7 million settlement in 2026. Courthouse News
