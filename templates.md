Subject: Eligibility question before applying: teen-led heat shut-off policy project

>
> I'm [name], a [grade] at [school] in California, and I'd like to apply for fiscal
> sponsorship for a teen-led project. Before I apply, I'd like to check that it fits.
>
> The project publishes a free website showing, for each US state, whether the state stops
> utilities from cutting off a household's electricity for unpaid bills during extreme heat,
> and how many dangerous-heat days each rule actually covers. It is built from each state's
> legal text and ten years of weather data.
>
> I'd like to confirm four things in writing:
> 1. Can the project, alongside its education work, ask state legislators to support specific
>    heat shut-off bills? This would be nonpartisan, within 501(c)(3) lobbying limits, and never
>    for or against any candidate.
> 2. Can it file public comments with state utility commissions?
> 3. Can it have student-led teams in other states, all run under this one HCB account?
> 4. If the project later becomes its own nonprofit, who keeps the name, website and remaining
>    funds?
>
> I'm under 18, and a parent will co-sign the agreement.
>
> Thank you,
> [name]

## 2. One-page chapter agreement

> **[Project name] chapter agreement**
>
> Chapter: [state / school]. Chapter lead: [name, grade]. Adult adviser: [name, email].
> Term: one school year, renewed each fall.
>
> 1. **Mission.** The chapter works to get [state] a rule that stops power shut-offs during
>    dangerous heat, using the project's state data.
> 2. **Status.** The chapter is a team within [Project name], not a separate organization. It
>    does not open its own bank account. Money goes through the project's fiscal sponsor, and
>    expenses are reimbursed against receipts.
> 3. **Name and speaking.** The chapter may use the name "[Project name] – [state]". Only the
>    project's published data may be cited as the project's numbers. Any position on a specific
>    bill is cleared with the project lead first. The chapter never supports or opposes a
>    candidate.
> 4. **Accounts.** Chapter social accounts are created with a project email, and the project
>    lead has access.
> 5. **Safety.** Each member under 18 has a signed parental consent form. At in-person events
>    with minors, two adults are present where possible (California AB 506 policy). Adults don't
>    send one-on-one private messages to minors. Photos of members are posted only with
>    permission.
> 6. **Check-in.** The lead files a short monthly update: actions taken, meetings, and
>    comments filed. A chapter that misses two in a row is listed as inactive.
> 7. **Honest counting.** The chapter reports active members, not sign-ups.
> 8. **Graduation.** Before leaving, the lead names a successor and hands over all records.
> 9. **Ending.** Either side can end this agreement in writing. Accounts and records go back to
>    the project.
>
> Signed: chapter lead / parent or guardian (if under 18) / adult adviser / project lead. Date.

## 4. Public comment to a utility commission (outline)

1. Proceeding number and title.
2. Who you are, and where you live.
3. What the current rule is, quoted, with a link.
4. The gap, as a sourced number: for example, the share of alert days the rule doesn't cover
   (`result.md`).
5. The specific change you're asking for: for example, a heat-index or weather-service-alert
   trigger, as the National Consumer Law Center recommends.
6. A comparison: California's July 2026 change cut uncovered alert days from 4.0 to 1.1 a year
   (`result.md`).
7. Before submitting, check the privacy setting. California's commission publishes your name
   and city unless you choose "private."
